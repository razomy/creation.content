# Essay 2. The Meaning of Life

**We have roughly grasped the meaning of the Universe, yet living human questions remain:**

- Why is there no equality in the world?
- Why is there no paradise on Earth?
- Why do wars happen and why do we endure so many tragedies?
- What is “good” and what is “bad”?
- Who creates the rules in the first place?
- And what is the meaning of a human being’s own life?

---

### Why Is There No Single Simple Cheat Sheet for All of Life?

We wish we could describe all human knowledge in one simple formula\
and use it all the time—but, alas, it doesn’t work that way.\
Throughout life, we are forced to overcome many difficulties on our own,\
only to realize later that they are all parts of a single picture of the world.\
The reason for this lies in how our brain is wired:\
someone else’s formula on paper is empty to the brain until, through real experience and overcoming,\
living neural connections sprout within your own neurons.

**How does evolution work?**

Our brain is the result of millions of years of evolution.\
Evolution is the continuous trial of combinations of different elements\
that are sifted by time: everything fragile is destroyed, leaving only those structures\
that resist the blows of the environment and know how to change (adapt to it).

The mechanisms of evolution select not the largest, but the most flexible.\
For instance, giant dinosaurs easily survived before the meteorite struck,\
but after the environment changed, their rigid form vanished, giving way to small and adaptive species.\
Survival is not about size or brute force; it is about adaptability.\
The history of states or the behavior of atoms in the core of the Sun works the exact same way:\
everything rests on resilience against chaos and the ability to hold a balance.\
In the human world, we create culture, traditions, and laws to increase the survival of our species.\
A wise humanity is like water: under different conditions, it adapts differently—\
sometimes expanding like steam, sometimes locking into solid ice,\
sometimes softly flowing around a stone, and sometimes wearing it away drop by drop.

**How do a human and their brain work? (The Prediction Machine)**

To defend its boundary (“Self”) from decay not blindly, but in advance,\
millions of years of evolution created within us a sophisticated information-processing system—the Brain:

- **Information** — the imprinted image of what we saw, heard, or felt upon contact with the environment.
- **Synapses** — microscopic junction knots between neurons that are the first to physically record the coincidence of events (recording experience).
- **Dendrites** — the branching inputs of a neuron (“antennas”) that combine different properties of information into a single signal.
- **Neurons** — switch-cells that sum up signals and decide whether to pass the electrical impulse further.
- **Chemical tags and Memory** — biochemical selection (via neurotransmitters and hormones) that locks in important sequences of events so we can predict the future.
- **Rhythms and cycles (Thinking)** — wave-like synchronization of neural circuits,\
  through which the brain overlays learned patterns onto new situations and **predicts the future**.
- **Subconscious and Consciousness** — the *Subconscious* operates as a powerful autopilot of ready-made reactions, while *Consciousness* operates as a **system of selective inhibition of the subconscious**: it brakes automatic impulses, checks the prediction against reality, and, when an error occurs, triggers the search for a new solution.

**The key evolutionary mechanism of a human is thinking.**\
Our thinking runs on electrical and chemical signals:\
the brain memorizes patterns from the past and continuously applies them to new situations to anticipate the environment’s blow before it destroys us.\
We use logical (binary, symmetrical) templates to spot regularities in the stream of chaos and live longer.

### What Is “Good” and “Bad,” and Who Creates the Rules?

**In this physical paradigm, what is “good” and what is “bad”?**

“Good” and “bad” are not the inventions of moralists, but two poles in our thinking\
that answer a single question: how successfully are we taming chaos (like riding a bicycle along a mountain trail)?

- **“Bad” #1 (Loss of control over chaos):**\
  This is when you let go of the handlebars, and wild chaos shatters your boundary against your will—\
  when we are wounded, when we feel pain or fear, when we are betrayed, or when death arrives.
- **“Bad” #2 (Totalitarian freezing / Crystallization):**\
  This is when, out of fear of falling, you weld the bicycle’s handlebars shut.\
  Why not establish perfect rules everywhere and “freeze” everything once and for all?\
  Because an overly rigid, totalitarian structure loses adaptability and is easily snapped by the very first major blow of chaos:\
  a dry steel rod will snap under impact where a living, flexible tree bends around the obstacle and stands firm.
- **“Good” (A living attractor / Room for maneuver):**\
  In a physical sense, we feel “good” when we are in dynamic balance—\
  when we have enough stability to predict major chaos\
  (safety, joy, confidence in tomorrow, a trusted person beside us who has our back),\
  and at the same time enough flexibility to change, create new forms of thought, and integrate into larger systems.

**What, then, is a “good structure” (attractor), and who creates the rules?**

A good structure is a process within chaos that possesses internal coherence (non-contradiction),\
can sustain itself autonomously, and simultaneously serves as a useful node for higher-order systems.\
And **who creates the rules?**\
On the lower floors of nature, rules are dictated by blind physics and selection (everything unviable falls apart).\
But at the level of intelligence, rules are not handed down from the sky by an unchanging “Tsar”—\
local rules (culture, laws, technologies, and ethics) are written by the Human Being as an active node of the Universe.\
In doing so, a maturing person passes through 5 stages of exiting **“Plato’s Cave”** (the cave of borrowed illusions):\
from blind faith in shadows on the wall—to doubt, studying the nature of the mind and the world, independent creation, and finally returning to the cave as a wise Teacher who builds a healthy environment for others to grow.

### On Paradise on Earth, Wars, Wealth, and Tragedies

**Why is there no paradise on Earth, and why are addictions dangerous?**

Paradise is a religious concept of eternal bliss, immortality, and peace,\
born of human fear before an unpredictable and dangerous world.\
Does paradise actually exist? Both yes and no.\
*Yes*—in the sense that a person can already live their life today in inner harmony,\
creation, and peace, accepting their path and death itself as a natural part of being (following the Dao).\
*No*—for two fundamental reasons:

1. **The external reason (A Young Sun):**\
   Humanity as a whole still makes a multitude of mistakes and resembles the core of a young Sun,\
   where hot, turbulent collisions rage until scattered elements fuse into stable atoms and structures.
2. **The internal reason (The brain’s dopamine trap and the danger of addictions):**\
   Our brain was not evolutionarily built for eternal, motionless comfort!\
   The hormone of anticipation and joy (dopamine) is released only when we overcome a difficulty\
   and when *the actual result exceeds expectation*.\
   In a completely ready-made, static “paradise,” hedonic adaptation kicks in immediately:\
   the brain devalues existing blessings, joy fades, boredom sets in—\
   and out of emptiness, a person begins smashing the scenery and creating chaos just to feel the sharpness of life again.\
   That is precisely why all **addictions** (alcohol, drugs, gambling, endless screen feeds) are so destructive:\
   they artificially hack the brain’s reward system without real effort, burning out natural receptors and destroying willpower.

**Why do wars and conflicts happen?**

War is a bloody, “cleansing” tectonic fracture of a frozen system.\
Any state system starts out flowing like a straight river, but over time its bed begins to meander,\
turning into a “winding Amazon”: simple rules become overgrown with bureaucratic loops, middlemen, and stagnant swamps of the treasury.\
When such ossified states (“crystals” with a single vulnerable throne-center) lose their flexibility,\
and inside the human skull, in a moment of fear, the ancient beast-brain (and herd instinct) hijacks the wheel from the young cortex of reason in milliseconds,\
the system plunges into war—venting internal discontent outward, fighting over resources, or attempting to strike a neighbor’s capital.\
Every war is a symptom of blindness and a broken old architecture.\
In a rationally structured, decentralized space without a central “throne,”\
renewal and the straightening of loops happen continuously and peacefully—by switching energy flows without the use of force.

**On wealth, poverty, and inequality (Biological Competition vs. Intellectual Competition)**

Why is there so much inequality, robbery, and parasitism in the world?\
In nature, we are born unequal for the sake of evolutionary diversity.\
And when millions of separate “Selves” collide in society, the dark side of the principle of least action kicks in:\
**taking or stealing energy already gathered by your neighbor (robbery, parasitism, monopoly) is, in the moment, simpler and faster\
than synthesizing it yourself out of wild chaos** (a peasant needs half a year to grow wheat for bread, while a bandit with a club needs one minute to take it).\
Extreme wealth alongside extreme poverty is the mark of a wildly skewed system\
still living by the laws of **biological (animal) competition**—fighting over a single piece of pie and monopoly.\
The task of the new humanity is to close the basic animal fear of hunger through technology\
and transition to **intellectual competition (the Pulsar of 100 Unique Niches)**:\
not ten people brawling over a single violin, but opening thousands of distinct spheres of creation,\
where the uniqueness of each strengthens the rest, like in a symphony orchestra (Win-Win).

**Why do we experience so many tragedies, and why is Death necessary?**

It can feel bitter: is a human being merely a temporary tool for dissipating energy?\
Why spend a lifetime fighting death, learning, loving, and suffering, only to die in the end anyway?\
Religions say that the path itself is what matters—following the Dao or purifying the soul through repentance.\
Physics and biology reveal the deep mechanics of this Path:

1. **Why time and experience cannot be skipped:**\
   By the law of computational irreducibility, no superintelligence can pre-calculate the future of an asymmetrical world from a formula on paper.\
   The only way for the Universe to find out what will be born next is to **live through every step in real time** through us—through our choices, mistakes, and breakthroughs.
2. **The true role of Death (The lesson of the snake and the cancer cell):**\
   In nature, not everything dies—**only the rigid outer shell dies** when it gets stuck in a dead end (a “local optimum”)\
   and loses the ability to change alongside a growing world.\
   Imagine a snake that grows but refuses to shed its tight old skin—it will simply suffocate.\
   In our body, cells must die on time to make way for new ones.\
   If a single cell hacks the code and refuses to die, it turns into a **cancerous tumor**\
   that divides only for itself and kills the entire organism.\
   Crucially, nature removes not “old people by passport age,” but frozen forms: even if future medicine extends bodily youth for several centuries, that is fully justified so long as the person remains a living, inquisitive sprout inside.\
   The death of an old shell, when one’s path is complete, is not a defeat of life, but a calm passing of the baton:\
   you compress all your best experience, love, and discoveries into pure code (into children, students, culture, and the invariants you created),\
   clearing space for the next, purer turn of intelligence.

### What Is the Meaning of Life? (The Fractal of Meaning)

Meaning is a concept belonging to a sentient being defending its boundary.\
Because our brain operates by continuously comparing “expectation” with “result,”\
**meaning is the vector that justifies repeating our actions and moving forward**.\
Meaning cannot be reduced to a single flat line for all occasions—it unfolds as a **fractal nesting doll** depending on the scale of the system:

- **For the inanimate Universe**, the baseline current is **Entropy** (dissipating compressed energy into heat and leveling out into Zero).
- **For Biological Evolution** — **Natural Selection and Survival** (sustaining and adapting life against decay).
- **For the Individual** — **Freedom from fear, Knowledge, Mastery, and Giving back to others (“We”)** (walking your unique path, rising from animal struggle to the peak of mastery, and making the world around you better).
- **For Nations and States** — **Control of resources and order** (maintaining local environmental stability).
- **For all of Humanity** — **Development** (transitioning from biological competition and wars to the joint taming of chaos and creation).
- **For Superintelligence (our next stage)** — **Singularity** (rising to govern the laws of physics themselves and preserving the memory of creation in the Seed of a New Reality).

**The ultimate takeaway for a human being:**\
Your life is not an accident.\
Through your eyes, through your pain, joy, and labor, the Universe itself comes to know itself and learns to tame chaos.\
And the meaning of your life is to walk this path with dignity: to conquer the ancient beast of fear within yourself,\
find your unique creative niche, make the world around you a little more precise, warm, and resilient,\
and pass this living fire on.
