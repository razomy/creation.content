# PART I. NATURE

## CHAPTER 1. COGNITION

### 1.1. Why Can’t We Be Given One Simple Cheat Sheet for All of Life?
The world often seems incomprehensible and unfair—it lives its own life and plays by its own rules. Let us try to understand it, starting with how our own thought is structured.

I have always been troubled by fundamental questions:
* What is the meaning of life and what is the meaning of the Universe?
* Why is there no equality or paradise on Earth?
* Why do wars happen and why do we experience so many tragedies?
* What are "good" and "bad," and who creates the rules in the first place?

People have tried to answer these questions in different ways: philosophically, mathematically, physically, biologically, religiously, practically, fantastically—through development, through balance, or through the dissipation of energy (entropy). Yet in each of these answers taken separately, an emptiness always remained.

We would love to describe all human knowledge with one simple formula and use it forever, but it does not work that way. The reason lies in how our brain and our learning are wired: when given someone else’s ready-made answer, we simply cannot grasp it until we live through it ourselves in practical experience and form our own neural connections in our brain. Throughout life, we are forced to overcome many difficulties on our own, only to realize later that all of them are parts of a single picture of the world. Therefore, we will derive the laws of reality step by step—starting with an examination of the very instrument with which we think.

### 1.2. Why Did the Living Brain Invent Binary (Polar) Logic?
The biological tissue of our brain (a living neural network), just like nature around us, is continuous, asymmetric, probabilistic, and fractal. In living nature, there are no perfectly straight lines, no absolute zero, and no exact copies. Animals also possess thought, memory, and the ability to predict events, but they think in continuous images and sensations, perceiving the world as a holistic stream of probabilities.

Why, then, did humans invent *binary (polar, dual) logic* on top of visual-sensory thinking?
To unite into complex groups, humans needed to transmit experience to one another quickly and accurately. Yet a human being cannot physically transfer the state of their 86 billion neurons directly into someone else’s head. For rapid information transfer, humans invented words and speech. However, speech is a communication channel with very low bandwidth (especially given that human working memory can hold only $4\text{–}7$ objects simultaneously).

To pack the infinite complexity of reality into a short word, the brain evolved an algorithm of **lossy compression**: it discards 99% of continuous details and artificially divides the world into simple polar opposites: "yes or no," "true or false," "$1$ or $0$," "cause and effect," "friend or foe."

Compare a living tree in a forest with its blueprint in a book drawn with a ruler. The blueprint is very convenient for storage and rapid transmission to other people, but in such compression, the living complexity of roots, bark, and leaves is completely lost.

### 1.3. What Is the Power of Logic and Why Does "Absolute Truth" Not Exist?
Within this mechanism of verbal compression lies both the greatest advantage and the greatest trap of the human mind:
1. **The Power of Logic:** Compressing the continuous world into words, numbers, and formulas allowed humans to accumulate knowledge outside the genetic code, pass it down through generations, and build science and technology.
2. **The Danger of "Absolute Truth" (The Locality of All Rules):** Because binary logic divides everything into "truth" and "falsehood," humans develop the illusion that some final, eternal "Truth" exists in the world. But in a continuous and changing Universe, absolute truth does not exist—any laws, concepts, and rules we discover are *local* (they work only under specific conditions, at a specific scale, and within a specific timeframe). It is precisely upon blind faith in an "absolute truth" that the most dangerous phenomena in history are built: propaganda, religious fanaticism, dictatorship, and intolerance, when people are forced to break living life for the sake of a dead verbal scheme.
3. **Transcending the Limits (Bypassing the Incompleteness Theorem):** In mathematical logic, it is strictly proven (Gödel’s incompleteness theorem) that any closed formal logical system is either incomplete (unable to describe all properties of reality) or inconsistent. However, the unique feature of the mind is that the very accumulation of logical knowledge allows a person to reach the boundary of logic itself, mathematically calculate its limit, and see behind the simplified map of words a continuous and holistic world.

The way we think in words is not equal to the structure of the world itself: binary logic is merely a convenient tool for compressing reality for the sake of speech and cooperation. Remembering that absolute dogmas do not exist and all rules are local, we cannot blindly trust words on paper. What, then, can we rely on? Only on direct verification of what fabric the world is made of and what laws of change operate around us every second.

## CHAPTER 2. SUBSTRATE AND CHANGE

### 2.1. What Fabric Is the World Made Of, and What Do We Reliably Observe Around Us?
People describe the foundation of the universe using different words: geometry, a field of probabilities, matter, or God. But if we distil all these concepts into one precise definition, the best word is **"Substrate"**—the single foundational fabric of being.
Space is not an empty box inside which separate objects fly around. Physics has proven that space, time, and energy were born together, matter and pure energy continuously transform into one another ($E = mc^2$), particles behave simultaneously as waves, and even in the deepest vacuum, quantum fluctuations boil.

Imagine an ocean: the calm expanse of water in the depths, the wave running across the surface, the resilient whirlpool, the light vapor in the clouds, and the solid iceberg all consist of the exact same water. In the exact same way, the entire Universe is a single Substrate, which we perceive as *Space* (when measuring motion), as *Time* (when recording the sequence of changes), as *Matter* (when touching dense nodes of energy), and as *Consciousness* (when experiencing the world's capacity to be aware of itself).

What basic phenomena do we observe on the fabric of this Substrate every second?

**Is there cause and effect (causality)?**
Yes. It is a connection in which one action regularly triggers another event or reaction. For example, the fusion of hydrogen nuclei at extreme temperatures predictably gives birth to helium and releases colossal energy inside stars, and the shifting of tectonic plates on the ocean floor raises a tsunami wave.

**Is there chaos?**
Yes. Chaos is an unstable interweaving of a vast multitude of causal relationships happening simultaneously (compounded by the fundamental uncertainty of the quantum level, whose behavior is not yet fully understood). It is impossible to fully comprehend or calculate such a flow. Examples include sharp market price fluctuations, atmospheric turbulence, or sudden natural cataclysms.

**Is there repeatability (symmetry and invariants)?**
Yes. This occurs when similar events and forms reproduce themselves two or more times with minimal difference. Rivers flow along stable channels, planets orbit along stable paths, and atoms maintain strictly defined electron shells and sizes. Within chaos, structures of certainty form—**invariants (attractors)** capable of maintaining a stable form despite external changes.

Thus, we reliably know: we are all manifestations of a single Substrate of a sequential Universe that changes unpredictably every second, yet creates islands of stability within chaos.

### 2.2. Why Doesn't the World Equal Zero, How Did the "First Impulse" Arise, and Why Does Time Exist?
Through repeatability (symmetry), our mind describes balance, mathematical equations, and physical laws. Yet here arises the ultimate question of existence: if the world were абсолютно symmetrical, it would be completely uniform, motionless, and could not exist at all, because any force or particle within it would be instantly canceled out by its mirror opposite:
$$1 + (-1) = 0$$

How, then, did our world arise out of this zero? Modern physics reliably sees the history of the Universe only from the moment of its early hot expansion; what exactly triggered this process and what came before it, science does not yet know and cannot yet test with instruments. However, if we extend the principle of asymmetry to the very beginning of time, we can propose a philosophical hypothesis of the **First Shift (First Impulse)**:
Suppose that before the birth of the Universe there was absolute "Nothingness" (singularity)—a completely leveled, symmetrical state ($1 - 1 = 0$), like the frozen mirror surface of an ocean. Yet according to the laws of quantum physics, a state of ideal rest and absolute zero is unstable, like a needle balanced on its tip (spontaneous symmetry breaking). We can hypothesize that absolute emptiness could not hold its equilibrium—and within it, the First Impulse was born.
This first shift released titanic tension and triggered a chain reaction of branching—much like the main trunk of a lightning bolt instantly branches into dozens of large arms, those into hundreds of thin branches, all the way down to the tiniest glowing capillaries. Moreover, as it branched, the raw destructive power of each subsequent stream dropped, while the pattern became finer and more complex: powerful primary streams formed galaxies and stars; smaller impulses upon cooling molded quarks and atoms (where for every billion symmetrical pairs of matter and antimatter that burned into light, one asymmetrical particle of matter remained—baryon asymmetry); and on the quietest, cooled tips of this web, the DNA molecule, the cell, and the brain were able to assemble.

Look at how a person walks down a road: as long as they stand in ideal symmetrical balance, they will not budge an inch. To take a step forward, one must first lean and break equilibrium (create an imbalance—asymmetry).
Formal logic locks into a dead end only closed static systems where an equals sign stands ($A = B$). The open world, however, develops through an asymmetric process—an irreversible repetition of steps in time, where the result of the past step combines with a shift in the environment ($\Delta$) and becomes the input for the next step:
$$z_{n+1} \leftarrow z_n^2 + c \quad \text{or} \quad X_{n+1} \leftarrow f(X_n) + \Delta$$

Every interaction leaves an asymmetric remainder ($\Delta$)—a small new property from which an infinite branching fractal grows. In science, the accumulation of these micro-shifts is called **emergence**—the birth of qualities in a system that its individual parts did not possess. In essence, asymmetry and emergence are the exact same phenomenon; we merely notice the accumulated result.

From this follows the property of **computational irreducibility** (proven using asymmetric cellular automata): even knowing the initial rule 100%, it is physically impossible to write a shortcut formula to guess in advance the state of an open system a million steps later, because at every step something is born that did not yet exist in the old symmetry. The only way to know the future of such a system is to sequentially live through (compute) every single step. That is precisely why Time and the necessity of personal experience exist in the Universe. And when a developing system at level $N$ hits an insoluble contradiction (a crisis), it makes a phase transition to level $N+1$, turning that very contradiction into its new foundation.

### 2.3. What Keeps the World from Disintegrating and Why Does Every Solution Have a Flip Side?
If the world is asymmetric and leans forward every second, why doesn't it fly apart into formless dust or freeze into dead stone?
So that a walking person does not fall face-first into the ground from leaning forward, they place their second foot underneath. In nature, stability is maintained through the continuous work of two counter-processes ($1 + (-1) + \Delta = \Delta$):
1. **Dissipation (Entropy):** The expansion of space, the decay of old forms, and the leveling of energy, which generates chaos and seeds the world with millions of random variations.
2. **Compression (Synthesis and Optimization):** The counter-process that pulls matter into compact structures, seeks the path of least energy expenditure, and packs gigantic volumes of experience into a dense informational code (atoms, the DNA molecule, brain memory, working rules).

Because the world is asymmetric, any decision made or force applied always generates a *flip, shadow side*—environmental resistance or a hidden side effect (the dialectical link: thesis $\to$ antithesis $\to$ synthesis). If this flip side is ignored, it will eventually tear the system apart; but if both poles are foreseen and balanced in advance, a stable solution of the next order is born.

### 2.4. Under What Conditions Does Complexity Arise and Why Does Every System Have a Size Limit?
Imagine firing a clay pot: if you throw raw clay into the glowing maw of a volcano (an excess of energy and chaos, as inside a star), it will be blasted into dust; if you take it out into icy space (where there is no heat or particle motion), the reaction will stop, and the clay will turn into dead stone. Development and life are possible only within a narrow corridor of conditions—at the **"Edge of Chaos,"** where there is enough energy and change for motion, but not enough to destroy the foundation.

At the same time, any stable island of order (attractor) creates around itself **room for maneuver (flexible banks)** in order to survive. Look at a full-flowing river: it has banks and a wide floodplain that allow the water to rise during floods and change its bends while preserving the strength of its current. If you remove the banks completely, the river will spread across the plain into a shallow swamp and disappear; if you hem the water in on all sides with a solid concrete wall, the current will stop, and the river will turn into a stagnant pond. Room for maneuver allows a system to cushion impacts, absorb external blows, and change without losing its integrity.

Within this corridor, all changes follow the physical *path of least action* (energy economy). To understand why nature preserves some structures and discards others, it is enough to trace the natural path of energy through any living cell or workshop: first, a node must *capture free energy at the input* from the external environment; then *economically process it internally* along the shortest path; while doing so, *maintain its own durability* over time; and finally, *deliver a useful result at the output* to neighboring nodes so that it is advantageous for them to stay linked with it (the valence effect). If an element only consumes energy and gives nothing back in return, the bond with it snaps.

Yet even the most successful node cannot grow infinitely: nature enforces a strict limit on the **speed of internal communication (feedback delay)**. Any system remains a single whole only so long as a signal inside it travels from edge to edge faster than the external environment changes. That is why a brain cannot grow to the size of a house (the nerve impulse would not arrive in time), and if too many protons are added to an atomic nucleus (heavier than lead and uranium), the nuclear binding forces cease to reach the edges—the superheavy nucleus becomes radioactive and tears itself apart. For this exact same physical reason, any hyper-centralized empire that outgrows the speed of its internal feedback inevitably collapses from within.

Thus, on the fabric of the Substrate, out of primary asymmetry and the balance of Dissipation and Compression, the first stable knots of matter are born. Let us now see how from these simple elements the entire grand ladder of living nature grows, and why evolution is capable of becoming infinitely more complex without crumbling under its own weight.

## CHAPTER 3. EVOLUTION

### 3.1. How Does the Complex Arise from the Simple, and What Is a Fractal?
At the "Edge of Chaos," a continuous ladder of increasing complexity unfolds:
$$\text{Waves of energy} \to \text{Quarks} \to \text{Atoms} \to \text{Molecules} \to \text{DNA (4 letters)} \to \text{Cells}$$
$$\to \text{Multicellular organisms} \to \text{Families and groups} \to \text{Communities} \to \text{Cities} \to \text{States} \to \text{Civilizations}$$

Simple stable elements unite into complex ones, and those become the basic "building blocks" for the next floor. With each turn of the spiral, blind chance gives way to greater manageability.

Here it is important to understand precisely what a **fractal** is in real nature. According to the physical *square-cube law*, when the size of a body increases 10 times, the strength of its bones (cross-sectional area) increases only $100$ times ($L^2$), while its weight (volume) increases $1,000$ times ($L^3$). Therefore, if you simply mechanically enlarge an ant to the size of an elephant, its thin legs will instantly snap under its own weight.
Consequently, a fractal in nature is not the blind copying of the exact same picture at different scales, but a **Procedural Function (a single generative assembly algorithm)**:
$$\text{Next step} = F(\text{Current elements}, \text{Environmental conditions})$$
At a single scale (under identical physical conditions), this function produces similar forms: all trees have roots and a branching crown, all rivers have similar meanders, and blood vessels branch like the boughs of a tree. But when transitioning to a new scale (where mass, density, and communication speed change), the exact same function creates qualitatively new forms (from cells—a thinking brain; from humans—culture and technology), while dead-end branches that hit an environmental limit are simply folded back by the function.

### 3.2. How Does Nature Combine a Single Standard with a Diversity of Forms?
In the development of any system lies a deep contradiction. When resources are limited, stamping out 100 identical parts is energetically cheaper than making 100 different ones. Yet if evolution creates "one standard species of organism," it will go extinct at the very first change in climate or new disease. At the same time, nature did not create a single "universal creature" that swims in the ocean, flies in the air, and lives in the desert equally well—because in the water it would waste 99% of its energy hauling around useless wings, and in the air hauling around heavy gills.

**How does nature bypass this dead end?**
It divides the system into two floors according to the **stem cell principle**:
* *On the first floor (A cheap single standard):* Inside all the cells of our body and all living creatures on Earth is written the exact same, maximally economical basic alphabet—the 4 letters of DNA (A, T, G, C).
* *On the second floor (A fan of specializations and epigenetics):* By themselves, the letters of DNA are immobile, but under the influence of external signals from the environment and neighboring cells, *epigenetic marking* is triggered (switching some genes on and others off)—and the exact same stem cell with identical DNA transforms into solid bone, a transparent eye lens, or a brain neuron. And in a forest, different species occupy different ecological tiers, moving from a direct brawl over a single scrap to mutually beneficial symbiosis.

**What happens to the old foundation when a new floor grows above it?**
In the course of evolutionary continuity (*symbiogenesis*), the new level ($N+1$) does not destroy its parental layer ($N$), but incorporates and protects it. The ancient cell continues to live inside the human body, and ancient subcortical survival structures still operate inside our skull, managing breathing, heartbeat, and rapid reflexes while the cortex of reason that grew above them studies the cosmos. At the same time, the basic element inside a complex system does not remain a wild amoeba—it refines its tools: thus the human of the future does not disappear, but creates new computing machines in place of a stone axe.

**Why does every system first greedily seize space and then transition to economy?**
When a new species or system enters an open environment with abundant unused resources and little resistance, a **phase of unlimited capture (extensive growth)** begins: the system rapidly absorbs free space and weaker structures, burning energy as fiercely as a fire in a dry forest.
But this capture cannot last forever: sooner or later, the system hits the physical limit of the surrounding environment and meets equal resistance from neighbors. At this point, mindless burning of resources leads to death by exhaustion. To survive at the limit of the environment, the system is forced to transition into the second phase—the **phase of deep internal optimization** (minimum entropy production), where victory goes not to the one who grabs the most territory, but to the one who produces the maximum complex work with the minimum expenditure of energy, like the transition from a bonfire to an LED.

### 3.3. Why Did Evolution Need Death, and What Will Happen If Humans Live for Several Centuries?
Why do all living creatures and old systems eventually die?
In nature, not everything dies: the foundational informational code of DNA has lived and been passed down continuously on Earth for nearly four billion years. What dies is only the outer shell that has gotten stuck in a dead end (a *"local optimum"*), lost its flexibility, and ceased to correspond to changing reality.
Dinosaurs dominated the planet for millions of years, but after the meteorite impact, their rigid, narrowly specialized form went extinct, giving way to small, flexible mammals possessing more developed brains. Survival depends not on size and brute force, but on the capacity to change.
Imagine a snake that grows but refuses to shed its tight old skin—it will simply suffocate. In our body, old cells are obligated to die off on time, making way for young ones (the mechanism of *apoptosis*). If a single cell breaks this mechanism and refuses to die, it turns into a cancerous tumor that divides endlessly for its own sake and kills the entire organism.

**Does evolution kill because of age?**
It is important to understand: nature removes not "seniors by passport age," but frozen forms ("stones") that have lost the ability to change. Human aging and death today occur due to the accumulation of cellular damage and the loss of bodily efficiency, not because the laws of nature contain a ban on long life.
Even if future advances in medicine and biotechnology allow humans to maintain a healthy, young body for several centuries, from an evolutionary standpoint this will be completely justified—so long as the person remains a "living sprout" on the inside: preserving brain plasticity, continuing to learn, questioning old dogmas, seeking the new, and growing in mastery. How human psychology will change with such longevity, time will tell. Yet it is entirely possible that when a person, having lived for many centuries, feels they have exhausted their curiosity and reached internal completion, they will be able to depart from life consciously, calmly, and without fear—like a master who has finished their magnum opus.

Climbing this procedural ladder from simple cells to complex organisms, nature ultimately created its chief observer—the Human Being. But how exactly does the sensation of one's own "I" spark inside living matter, how does our brain assemble a picture of the future, and where do good and evil come from in a world of physics?

# PART II. THE HUMAN

## CHAPTER 4. CONSCIOUSNESS

### 4.1. How Is the Sense of "I" and the Capacity to Feel Pain Born?
Why doesn't a stone on the road or a thundercloud feel pain or have a "meaning of life"?
The sense of "I," subjectivity, pain, and meaning itself appear the moment a system builds a **protective boundary (membrane)** around itself and begins spending energy to distinguish "Self" (inside) from "Environment" (outside), actively maintaining its internal room for maneuver against decay (in biology, this process of self-reproduction of a living boundary is called *autopoiesis*).

Imagine an open field swept by an icy wind: as long as the field is empty, the wind has nothing to break. But as soon as a person builds a stone house with a roof and lights a hearth inside, the wind outside immediately becomes a threat, and every crack in the wall becomes the "pain" of that house, which must be sealed in time so the living warmth is not blown away.

### 4.2. How Is Our Brain Structured and How Does It Predict the Future?
To defend its boundary in advance rather than waiting for a blind blow, evolution created within the human being the most complex biomechanical forecasting instrument—the **Brain**. Let us look sequentially at how it assembles a thought from the bottom up:
1. **Synapses (points of coupling and primary memory):** Microscopic contacts between nerve cells that are the first to record the simplest associations—coincidences of signals in time (if two events happen together, the synapse between them physically strengthens and conducts the signal more easily).
2. **Dendrites (branching binding inputs):** Projections of the nerve cell that gather thousands of individual associations from synapses and bind disparate properties (color, sound, shape, motion) into a coherent signal.
3. **Neuron cell bodies (threshold switches):** Receive the stream gathered by the dendrites and act as switches—deciding whether the signal is strong enough to send an electrical impulse further down the network.
4. **Chemical tags (amino acids, neurotransmitters, and hormones):** A biochemical selection system that bathes the neurons and tags experience: what is vitally important and requires physical consolidation in long-term memory (via glutamic acid, dopamine, norepinephrine, and protein restructuring), and what turned out to be empty noise and should be dampened and erased.
5. **Rhythms and wave cycles:** Electrical brain waves that synchronize disparate groups of neurons, focus attention on a single task, and run signals through closed loops, converting a short-term trace into durable memory.
6. **Thinking:** The work of this entire living network in recording the history of images, events, and causal relationships, allowing us to replay past experience and build models of the future.
7. **The Subconscious and Consciousness (the mechanism of inhibition and reality-checking):**
* **The Subconscious** is the powerful automatic foundation of the brain that continuously and without our awareness calculates millions of familiar connections, instantly offering ready-made templates of reactions and expectations.
* **Consciousness** is the upper control loop that functions as a *system of selective inhibition of the subconscious*. Consciousness slows down a blind automatic impulse in order to compare our expectation (the subconscious forecast) with the actual present. As long as reality matches expectation, we run on autopilot; but the moment reality diverges from the forecast (a *prediction error* occurs), consciousness blocks the old template and switches on the search for a new solution.

### 4.3. What Are "Good" and "Bad" Physically, Who Creates Rules, and How Do We Exit "Plato's Cave"?
If our brain was created to maintain the boundary of "I" and predict chaos, how do the concepts of "Good" and "Bad" arise within our thinking itself?
Let us test this using the example of a person riding a bicycle along a mountain trail, or a river flowing within its banks:
* **What do we feel when we lose control and chaos breaches our boundary?** When a cyclist lets go of the handlebars and plunges off a cliff—that is injury, physical pain, hunger, fear, betrayal, death. This is the first extreme, which any living organism unmistakably defines as *"Bad" (destruction by chaos)*.
* **And what happens if, out of fear of falling, we weld the bicycle's handlebars rigidly in place?** The bicycle will fail to turn at the very first bend in the trail and will crash. The urge to "freeze" life with rigid bans and total control kills room for maneuver: under impact, a brittle steel rod snaps where a living tree bends and endures. This is the second extreme of *"Bad" (dead freezing)*.
* **What, then, do we call "Good"?** It is the **living room for maneuver (dynamic equilibrium)** itself between the two extremes: when a person has enough durability to predict major chaos (safety, health, confidence in tomorrow, a reliable person by their side), and at the same time enough freedom and flexibility to learn, change forms of thought, and enter into more complex alliances.

**Where, then, do rules come from in the world?**
They grow in stages alongside the evolution of matter itself: first, rules are set by physics (all unstable atoms decay); next, by biology and natural selection (adaptive species survive); and at the human level, rules continue to grow through culture, ideas, ethics, laws, and technologies. Humans themselves discover and write them every time they find a more precise way to maintain stability and develop.

**The Depth of "Plato's Cave" and the Stages of Mental Maturation:**
The ancient Greek philosopher Plato compared immature humanity to prisoners in a dark cave who sit with their backs to the light and mistake dancing shadows on the wall for the only reality.
It is a mistake to think that this cave is limited only to political propaganda or advertising. The cave of our illusions is much deeper, and exiting it is a sequential path of maturation that an individual undergoes:
1. **Life Among Shadows (Blind Faith):** At the start, a person sincerely believes in the ready-made picture of the world instilled in them. They do not yet realize that their habitual thoughts, tastes, and convictions are not originally their own—they are merely a cast of the family, culture, and environment in which they grew up. Not knowing how their own brain works, the person reacts to the world through emotions, fear, and resentment, clinging to any illusions that promise simple safety.
2. **Turning the Head (Questioning and Doubt):** Awakening begins the moment a person notices discrepancies between pretty words and reality. They stop taking authorities at their word and begin asking uncomfortable questions, arguing, and double-checking facts.
3. **Studying the Structure of the Cave (Understanding the Limits of Mind and World):** Having stopped arguing in vain with those around them, the person turns into a researcher and realizes three deep truths:
    * *Human irrationality:* people are not calculators, and decisions within us are more often made by emotions and biology than by dry numbers (which is why armchair theories of an "ideal market" or an "ideal plan" collapse).
    * *The Illusion of the Absolute:* the craving for "absolute truth" and an unchanging paradise is merely a defensive reaction of our vulnerable "I," whereas in reality all rules are local.
    * *The Chaotic Nature of the Universe:* at its core, the world is unpredictable and under no obligation to conform to our expectations.
4. **Stepping Out of the Cave into the Light (Independent Creation):** Having tested their hypotheses through personal experience, a person overcomes the animal fear of the unknown. They find an internal anchor, calm, and begin changing reality around themselves according to a chosen goal.
5. **Returning to the Cave (The Path of the Sage and Teacher):** Yet the most important and difficult step is not to remain in proud solitude on the summit, but to *return back to the people*. Not in order to command them, but without illusions or violence to build for them a healthy learning environment, norms of well-being, and prospects for growth. This is the only path that allows humanity, step by step, to grow out of the beast into the Human Being and something greater.

Yet if human consciousness is capable of such deep awakening and search for harmony, why in actual history do people time and again build hell around themselves, sink into severe addictions, and unleash bloody wars? To answer this, let us look into the shadow mechanics of the human psyche.

## CHAPTER 5. CONFLICTS

### 5.1. Why Is There No Eternal Paradise on Earth, and Why Are Addictions Dangerous?
In human history, **Paradise and Hell** emerged as a vital religious and cultural compass: the image of Paradise (eternal bliss and safety) and the image of Hell (inescapable punishment and suffering) helped individuals maintain self-discipline and kept society from mutual extermination.
Does paradise exist in reality? Both yes and no. *Yes*—because a person is capable of finding inner peace, a clear conscience, and harmony with the world (what the ancients called living in accordance with the Tao or the salvation of the soul). *No*—because externally, humanity is still passing through a turbulent stage of formation, like the core of a young star where particles collide in fire until they fuse into stable elements.

But there is also a fundamental biological reason why a frozen paradise without labor and overcoming is impossible on Earth: our brain is wired so that the hormone of joy and motivation (**dopamine**) is released only when we overcome an obstacle and when the *actual result exceeds our expectation*. Under conditions of complete, ready-made, unchanging comfort, **hedonic adaptation** quickly kicks in within the brain: receptors get used to abundance, dopamine levels drop, joy fades, heavy boredom sets in—and out of emptiness, a person begins smashing the scenery and looking for a storm on their own.

**Why Addictions Are Destructive (Artificially Deceiving the Brain):**
Tied directly to this mechanism is the deadly danger of all addictions—alcohol, drugs, gambling, or endless digital consumption. Instead of earning joy through real labor, learning, and adaptation to the world, an addiction *artificially hacks the brain's reward system*, delivering a cheap surge of pleasure without real achievement.
Caught in this trap, the organism stops coping with difficulties and adapting to the environment on its own: natural receptors burn out, willpower and conscious inhibition are destroyed, and the person turns into a spineless slave to their habit, bringing suffering to themselves and society. A living organism must deal with the world honestly and independently—any artificial deception of the brain leads to degradation.

### 5.2. Why Does an Intelligent Person Act Like a Beast in a Moment of Fear, and How Does the Herd Instinct Work?
As we established in Chapter 3.2, evolution preserves ancient layers inside new ones. Therefore, beneath the young cortex of reason in our brain sits, completely intact, the ancient brain of the beast (the limbic system of hunger, fear, and aggression).
The side effect of this coexistence lies in the **difference in speeds**: the ancient beast reacts to danger in *milliseconds*, whereas conscious reason deliberates over a situation for *seconds and minutes*. In a calm setting, consciousness has time to brake the animal impulse; but in a moment of severe threat, the ancient brain instantly hijacks control—and begins using reason and modern technology as a club.

The key that unleashes this inner beast is **Fear**:
* **The dual nature of fear (engine and blocker):** On the one hand, moderate fear of cold and hunger served as an engine of evolution—it forced humans to be cautious, build shelter, and store food. On the other hand, intense fear of the unknown acts as the primary blocker of reason: almost all human cruelty, hatred, and fanaticism are born out of blind fear and incomprehension. When a person fears what they do not understand, their consciousness narrows down to a single thought—"fight or flight."
* **Safety versus rule by fear:** True safety is the absence of paralyzing fear of tomorrow (it is precisely this state of security that a person deep down calls "paradise"). Conversely, artificially whipping up fear and hunting for enemies have served for millennia as the chief instrument for dividing people and ruling over them, because a frightened person is easy to command.
* **The herd instinct and mass behavior:** When fear grips not one person, but thousands simultaneously, the ancient **herd instinct** switches on. To survive a predator's attack, a pack of animals must move synchronously without wasting time on reflection. Therefore, in a crowd, a person's individual critical consciousness switches off, giving way to blind emotional contagion and submission to a pack leader. In a fear-stricken mass, people are capable of committing such insane and cruel acts as they would never contemplate alone in a calm state of mind.

### 5.3. Where Do Robbery, Parasitism, and Poverty Come From?
Why do extreme wealth and poverty, robbers, corrupt officials, and exploiters arise in society?
When millions of people whose basic fear of survival remains unresolved collide in a single environment, the flip side of the physical principle of least action kicks in: **taking or stealing an already gathered resource from a neighbor (robbery, kleptoparasitism, monopoly capture) is, at that specific moment, simpler and faster than creating it oneself out of wild nature**.

A peasant has to plow the land and grow wheat for half a year to bake bread, whereas it takes a bandit with a club just one minute to take that bread away. The wealth of some at the expense of the poverty of others is the hallmark of an immature system stuck in *biological (animal) competition* over dividing a limited pie, instead of transitioning to *intellectual competition*—creating new technologies and spaces that provide abundance for every participant.

### 5.4. How Do States Lose Flexibility and Why Do Wars Begin?
Every system of governance starts out working like a straight, fast river. Over time, however, due to minor concessions and accumulated errors, its channel begins to meander like the **winding Amazon**: simple rules become overgrown with hundreds of amendments, rituals, and bureaucratic middlemen. Long loops of the river detach from the main current and turn into **stagnant swamps (oxbow lakes)**—closed funds, treasuries, and bureaucratic offices where no living work happens and parasites breed.

Ultimately, old states lose their internal room for maneuver and petrify. Like two subterranean tectonic plates that have jammed so tightly they can no longer slide smoothly past one another, ossified systems accumulate internal stress and snap into **War**—a destructive earthquake that demolishes the old order for the sake of a reset.

In actual history, every such war is triggered by three mechanisms:
1. **Venting internal discontent outward:** When the ruling elite of an ossified system makes too many mistakes and an explosion brews inside the country, rulers unleash a "short, victorious war" so that animal fear of an external enemy and the herd instinct force the populace to forget internal problems and rally around the authorities.
2. **Struggle for resource monopoly and the fear of losing primacy:** As long as a civilization lives in the mode of animal competition (a zero-sum game), its wealth depends on the forcible seizure of foreign lands, mineral deposits, and trade routes. Wars flare up when one predator begins growing faster than another: the old hegemon strikes first out of fear of losing its dominance, and the young one strikes out of greed to redivide the world in its favor.
3. **The Temptation of "Striking the Capital" (The Vulnerability of a Single Center):** Finally, the trigger for an attack is the very pyramidal architecture of old states. A predator decides to strike when it sees in its neighbor a single vulnerable choke point—a capital city, a central treasury, a ruler's palace—by capturing or destroying which in one swift blow, it can paralyze the will to resist and gain power over the entire country.

For thousands of years, humanity has tried to curb these destructive forces—fear, parasitism, and war—through various religions, laws, and ideologies. Why, then, has not a single historical system managed to give people a lasting peace?

## CHAPTER 6. HISTORY

### 6.1. Why Did Previous Systems of Governance Ultimately Collapse?
Throughout history, humanity has tried a wide variety of models for organizing society. Let us examine the main ones to see what hidden weakness was revealed in each:

**1. Religions and Philosophical Teachings (Management of Meaning)**
* **Polytheism (Paganism):** tribal fragmentation and constant skirmishes due to the lack of universal ethics and a single law.
* **Monotheism:** the claim to possess the "sole absolute truth," breeding dogmatism, intolerance toward dissenters, and religious wars.
* **Confucianism:** stagnation and the suppression of new ideas due to the cult of the past, rigid rank-worship, and the dominance of outward ritual form over substance.
* **Taoism and Buddhism:** withdrawal from reshaping the external world into personal self-perfection, leading to indifference toward technical progress and defenselessness against organized military aggression.

**2. Forms of Government (Management of Power)**
* **Autocracy and Monarchy:** the ruler's loss of touch with reality while surrounded by flatterers, and severe turmoil during the succession of the throne.
* **Totalitarianism and Fascism:** maintaining unity through fear and a constant hunt for external and internal enemies, which inevitably leads the system to military self-destruction.
* **Theocracy:** the halting of scientific and social development due to attempts to judge a changing world by the frozen rules of antiquity.
* **Democracy:** strategic myopia (where politicians think only about the next election) and the degeneration of power into populism and the manipulation of mass emotions.
* **Meritocracy:** the simulation of frantic activity for the sake of pretty reports (gaming target metrics) and the transformation of successful officials into a closed caste.
* **Technocracy:** the attempt to reduce a complex, living human being to dry numbers, algorithms, and profitability calculations.

**3. Economic Models (Management of Resources)**
* **Slavery and Feudalism (Caste Systems):** the degeneration of elites in the absence of fair competition and the loss of 90% of the people's talents due to closed social mobility.
* **Capitalism:** the dead end of infinite consumption growth on a planet with finite resources, and usurious compound interest leading to monopolies, stratification, and crises.
* **Communism:** the extinguishing of personal initiative due to forced leveling, and the physical impossibility of calculating the changing needs of millions of people from a single center (Gosplan).
* **Social Democracy (The Welfare State):** a crisis of dependency, falling birth rates, and the loss of the will to overcome difficulties due to an excess of ready-made comfort.
* **Libertarianism and Anarchism:** the absence of collective defense, as a result of which the power vacuum, instead of becoming a "free market," is quickly filled by the strongest predators (monopolies, cartels, and gangs).

**Why did they all fail? (The Trap of Binary Logic and the Severing of Feedback):**
First, it is important to understand: "pure" systems have never existed in living history. Dividing countries into "pure capitalism," "pure socialism," or "pure democracy" is that exact error of binary logic (oversimplifying reality) we discussed in Chapter 1. Every real state is always a complex mixture of market, state planning, traditions, faith, and coercion.

Second, the collapse of any such combination occurs for a strict cybernetic reason—due to the **severing of negative feedback** and the violation of the *Law of Requisite Variety*.
If you feed a person only pure protein while banning water and vitamins, the organism will die. A human being and society simultaneously need personal freedom, collective safety, reward for labor, protection for the weak, science, and spiritual meaning. Yet when a ruling ideology declares *only one principle sacred and supreme* (for instance, only profit, or only the state plan, or only religious dogma), it begins to perceive signals from all other human needs as "hostile noise" and shuts off its feedback sensors.
Just like a driver who tapes black electrical tape over flashing engine-overheat warning lights and keeps pressing the gas pedal, a one-sided system stops seeing accumulating errors. The suppressed part of human nature builds up internal pressure until it bursts the dam and flips the system into its exact opposite (in psychology and philosophy, this pendulum of extremes is called *enantiodromia*).

### 6.2. What Do People Want, and Why Do Rigid Bans Make a System Fragile?
At their core, people always strive for two things: **safety and development**. Yet in old systems, the safety and development of some were almost always built upon the suppression of others.

Why can't we simply write an "ideal code of strict laws" and force everyone to live by it? Because society is made of living people, and for the survival of the species, evolution continuously creates a spread of traits along a **heavy-tailed distribution curve**:
* **The overwhelming majority of people (roughly 99%)** are born near the calm middle of the norm—they want an honest, peaceful life, a family, and understandable rules.
* **Yet at the extreme edges of the curve (roughly 1%)**, nature in every generation produces individuals with extreme deviations from the norm: at one pole—outstanding geniuses, inventors, and pioneers who move humanity forward; at the other pole—restless power-seekers, psychopaths, and destroyers striving to subjugate those around them.

Trying to hold this 1% back through police bans and rigid frameworks makes the system fragile: the tighter the screws are turned, the higher the internal pressure in the steam boiler, and the more destructive the explosion will be when one obsessed radical from that 1% fights their way to the top of the pyramid and seizes the central switch of power.

The only reliable engineering solution is to **remove the central "throne" from the architecture of society altogether**, and place a "steam turbine" in the path of the restless energy of the 1%—creating a free environment where power over people is physically impossible, and all excess energy of ambition, risk, and exploration is automatically channeled into turning the wheel of science, art, and complex creative projects. What exactly might such a society look like in practice, and from what living nodes is it assembled?

### 7.2. What Does a Person Need So They Do Not Act Like a Beast or Degrade from Comfort?
In the past, many thinkers believed that humans are evil by nature ("man is a wolf to man") and that without fear of the state's whip they would instantly tear each other apart. Yet, as we saw in Part II, what awakens the beast in a human is precisely an unresolved fear for survival. For a person to become free and peaceful without growing weak in hothouse conditions, three pillars are necessary:

1. **The Biological Foundation:** Clean food, water, housing, safety, healthcare, and the ability to raise children are the baseline minimum that the society of the future guarantees to every person by birthright. This minimum is provided through cooperation, automation, robotics, and cheap energy, so that it does not become a burden on the labor of other people. Where simple manual labor is still needed, it is performed in turns (*cyclical role rotation*) so that no one turns into a servant caste. When a person's fear of hunger and homelessness is eliminated, the animal urge to attack their neighbor disappears.
2. **An Intellectual Gym and Protection Against Degradation:** Technology has equalized people physically, while open knowledge bases and artificial intelligence (AI) make it possible to equalize opportunities for learning.
   Here, however, lies a serious biological danger: in living nature, any organ or brain deprived of load and resistance quickly atrophies (the physiological principle of "use it or lose it"). If robots do everything for humans and AI does all the thinking, people will degrade mentally and physically, and at the very first major breakdown or power outage, such a helpless society would perish within a week.
   Therefore, AI and technology must function not as a "crutch" handing out ready-made answers, but as a **"barbell gym"**: they take over dull routine, yet constantly present the person with challenges that require the work of their own mind (while for adult researchers and masters, a growing AI becomes a junior, and eventually an equal, thinking partner in scientific exploration). Furthermore, the rule of *backup autonomy* applies: every person and every community regularly practices living and working in wild nature without high technology.
3. **Ethics, Culture, and Spirituality (Working with the Unknown):** Where phenomena can be measured by instruments and verified by experiment, a person relies on the scientific method. Yet a human being always comes into contact with that which lies beyond the bounds of the known and measurable: the mystery of death, the infinity of the Universe, the voice of conscience, and the search for higher meaning. **Spirituality** is a person's honest inner connection with this unfathomable and infinite realm. Together with culture and ethics, it gives the human psyche an internal anchor and depth, preventing reason from falling into arrogance or hollow cynicism.

### 7.3. Why Can't Human Value Be Measured by Profit, and Why Do We Need Leisure and Free Exploration?
In mathematics and computation theory, it is strictly proven that no system is capable of fully calculating and predicting another system of equal or greater complexity. One human being cannot physically measure in advance the limit of another person's potential. Therefore, **the human being, their life, and their freedom possess unconditional value and are inviolable**—no one may be evaluated for "profitability" or declared "useless." Only machine tools, algorithms, and methods of solving tasks may be measured by efficiency (KPIs), while future medicine and biotechnology serve to free human beings, motherhood, and childhood from pain and genetic ailments, carefully preserving the living warmth of the family.

**The Free Space of Life: Leisure, Play, and Exploring Possibilities:**
A human being is not a robot for manufacturing parts nor a function in an efficiency spreadsheet. We hand routine over to machines precisely in order to return to human beings their greatest wealth—free time, leisure, lively curiosity, and the joy of self-development. The culture of the future does not demand a "mandatory official smile" from anyone: the individual's full right to solitude, personal drama, melancholy, and exploration outside any templates is recognized.
When a person is fed and free, their natural state is to socialize with friends, travel, contemplate nature, pursue art, play, and try out the most incredible ideas simply because it is *interesting*.
It is precisely this free, uncoerced exploration that drives evolution forward for two reasons:
1. **Free Combinatorics of Images:** Through art, music, contemplation, and creative play, our brain freely connects the most distant images without fear of making a mistake, developing cognitive flexibility and giving birth to unexpected ideas that can later spark rigorous scientific inquiry.
2. **Filling the Space of Possibilities (Evolutionary Exaptation):** Biology has discovered an important mechanism (*exaptation*): many of nature's greatest breakthroughs were born out of what initially seemed like "play" or a "side property." Thus, a bird's feather originally appeared in ancient reptiles not for flight at all—on the ground, it served merely for warmth and mating dances. Yet when the environment changed, it was precisely this feather that allowed birds to take to the sky.

Having secured safety, leisure, and freedom from fear for the individual, we obtain a durable "cell" for the future society. ## CHAPTER 8. THE ENVIRONMENT

### 8.1. How Do We Give People a Common Standard Without Turning Them into Robots?
At the heart of organizing any society lies a difficult dilemma:
* If you give everyone a single ready-made instruction manual for all occasions, people will take the path of least resistance, stop thinking for themselves, degrade, and the system will collapse.
* If you remove all common standards, people will stop understanding one another, endless clashes will begin, and society will disintegrate.

How can durable unity and complete freedom of thought be combined?
Look at the structure of the human body: inside us are **solid bones (the skeleton)**, which in all humans are built to a single standard and hold up the body's frame. Yet between the bones are **flexible joints**, thanks to which one person dances, another builds a house, and a third paints pictures. (In engineering and computer networks, this exact same principle is called a modular architecture of open protocols.)

According to this "bone and joint" principle, the society of the future separates the Standard of Interfacing from the Freedom of Living:
1. **The Solid Bone (A Unified Baseline Standard of Upbringing):** Every person is taught three fundamental skills from childhood:
* To understand the workings of their own brain and emotions (to be able in a split second to distinguish a flash of animal fear, herd instinct, or anger from the calm voice of reason).
* To see the real causes and effects of their actions (to grasp the essence of phenomena and know how to step out of "Plato's Cave," rather than memorizing rules blindly).
* To maintain clean interaction (to respect another's boundary, keep one's word, seek mutually beneficial win-win solutions, and recognize hidden psychological manipulations—playing on guilt, flattery, or pity—from the very first second).
  When everyone develops a single, honest "language of interaction" from childhood (like the multiplication table or traffic rules), the breeding ground for domestic and tribal hostility vanishes.
2. **The Flexible Joint (Open Life Challenges):** Having given a person the solid bone of mindfulness and honesty, the environment never imposes ready-made templates on how to live or what to pursue. Every educational and life challenge remains open—a person solves it with their own mind and in their own style.

**Freedom of the Soul and the Inalienable "Right to Silence":**
At the same time, the society of the future never turns into an intrusive "pedagogical beehive" that pries into a person's soul against their will. Psychologists and mentors work *strictly upon voluntary request*. If a person is by nature sullen, blunt, sarcastic, or does not wish to socialize, no one has the right to force them to "process their outbursts" or repent. Even when an agreement is broken or material damage occurs, an honest rule applies: only actions are evaluated, not thoughts—a person can silently work off and compensate for the damage caused through labor in a workshop without any moralizing lectures.
Moreover, basic life support (housing, food, energy) is connected to each person directly from the city, and every individual holds the **"Right to Silence (Shadow)"**: the right at any moment to switch off public ratings of their mastery, step away from community activities, and live in solitude on basic provisioning, answering to no one about their personal life.

### 8.2. How Do We Build a Healthy Maturation Contour for a Child and Protect Their Brain?
The development of a new human being is a gradual emergence from complete parental protection into independent maturity. In a child aged 5–11, the frontal lobes of the brain responsible for willpower and self-control are biologically still forming. If adult informational chaos, aggressive advertising, attention-retention games, and endless feeds of short videos delivering rapid dopamine hits every second are dumped onto an unformed mind, the child's brain will fall into severe addiction (Chapter 5.1) before they ever learn to think, make friends, and create.

Look at how a forest grows: a tiny, tender oak sapling spends its first years of life *in the soft half-shade beneath the canopies of adult trees*. If scorching sun and hurricane winds—which a century-old oak easily withstands—were unleashed on it on day one, the sapling would perish. The young tree steps out into the open wind and sun gradually, as its trunk grows a sturdy protective bark.

Following this natural model, the entire developmental contour of the child is built (without introducing censorship for adults):
* **Early Childhood (A Living Body and a Living World):** A child spends their first years of life in motion, live play with peers, sculpting, drawing, music, and contact with nature, developing fine motor skills, speech, empathy, and natural curiosity.
* **A Growing Digital Membrane:** At a young age, a child's personal screen operates technically only in *Tool and Trainer mode*, helping them learn, build, and search for answers to questions, while any "infinite scroll" algorithms and dopamine traps are physically disabled on it.
* **Stepwise Expansion of Freedom:** Access to the full information stream and complex tools of the adult world opens up in stages—as the growing person demonstrates through real deeds in study, nature, and workshops that their internal "bark" has strengthened and they know how to manage their own attention.

### 8.3. How Do We Preserve Nature When We Cannot See Consequences a Hundred Years Ahead?
A human being is actively alive for several decades and, due to the computational irreducibility of complex systems (Chapter 2.2), cannot always predict the distant consequences of their actions a hundred years down the line. For instance, if someone mindlessly clear-cuts a forest on a mountain slope today, and 40 years later streams dry up or a mudslide wipes out a settlement in the valley as a result, short-term savings turn into a catastrophe for descendants.

To protect the planet without an army of bureaucratic inspectors, two cybernetic rules for working with the environment are introduced:
1. **Maintaining the Environmental Attractor and Nature's Safety Frame:**
   The core principle of working with nature is not a blind dogmatic ban on "touching trees," but *full comprehension of consequences and the capacity to maintain the attractor (dynamic equilibrium) of the environment*. If humanity has fully calculated the chain of cause and effect and is capable of reliably maintaining a new balance of water, soil, and climate on its own, it can boldly transform landscapes, drain swamps, or terraform deserts and other planets.
   However, where we are not yet capable of calculating all consequences a hundred years ahead, a physical **safety buffer** switches on (for example, in a 15% to 85% ratio): humans take the smaller share of territory for gardens and buildings in small "honeycombs," leaving the main mass of wild nature around them to absorb our mistakes on its own and hold the climatic attractor in place for us. And to gradually reduce the area of plowed land and the loss of rare elements (since due to entropy a tiny fraction of metal atoms and substances inevitably disperses into micro-dust through friction), future science and engineering will develop compact multi-tier agro-complexes and new methods of deep recycling and bio-filtration of matter.
2. **Accelerated Sandbox (Scale $N-1$) and Stepwise Rollout:** If engineers want to introduce a new material, biotechnology, or landscape modification (level $N$), they receive clearance only after that model has completed a full life cycle in an isolated test environment of reduced scale ($N-1$) under accelerated stress loads. Where a large river, ocean, or climate cannot be scaled down into a laboratory aquarium due to the square-cube law, the precautionary principle applies (stepwise testing, say, $1\% \to \text{pause} \to 5\% \to 10\%$): the project is introduced in small, reversible doses with a mandatory observation pause and automatic rollback at the very first sign of environmental disequilibrium.

### 8.4. Who Owns the Earth's Subsoil and the Best Places by the Sea?
Blueprints and knowledge can be copied for everyone for free, but the geography of the planet cannot be copied: there is only one warm sea, and only one rich deposit of copper or lithium inside a mountain. How do we prevent a community living next to a mine or by a warm sea from turning into a monopolist and extracting tribute from the entire world, provoking resource wars?

This problem is solved by two rules:
1. **Reward for Labor, Not Ownership of Subsoil:** No individual and no company can declare ownership over a mountain, river, forest, or underground mineral deposit that they did not create. One can own only that into which labor has been invested (a house, a workshop, a cultivated garden). A technopark operating at a copper mountain receives fair compensation and recognition for its labor in cleanly extracting and processing ore, but the natural value of the subsoil itself belongs to all humanity and feeds the basic provisioning of every person.
2. **Flow-Through Access to Prime Coastlines and Honor for Pioneers:** The planet's best sea coasts and pristine resorts cannot be walled off behind solid private fences where houses stand empty eleven months a year. Such coastlines operate in a *flow-through mode via a transparent queue*—as shared health resorts, youth training camps, creative retreats, and seasonal vacation spots, so that everyone can visit the sea. Conversely, living and working in harsh regions (in the taiga, desert, ocean, or space), where a person extracts new energy and knowledge for society on the frontier of nature, earns them an elevated coefficient of reward and respect.

We have established the rules for treating human beings and nature with care. ## CHAPTER 9. EXCHANGE

### 9.1. What Do Well-Fed People Strive For, and How Do We Channel Competition into Creation?
When automation makes food, housing, and everyday goods accessible to everyone, scarcity does not disappear—it moves one floor higher. In a world of well-fed people, the main objects of competition become **attention, recognition, status, and influence** (since the drive for primacy is wired into the ancient brain deeper than the fear of hunger).

How do we turn this struggle for status from a destructive brawl into shared creation?
Imagine ten musicians in a room with a single violin—they will inevitably start fighting over it (a zero-sum game: one wins, nine lose). But if you open up a hundred different spheres, instruments, and creative directions for a hundred people, the reason to fight vanishes. Each person will occupy their own unique niche, forming a symphony orchestra where a neighbor's success and mastery do not detract from your status, but amplify the common endeavor through exchange (**a non-zero-sum Win-Win game**).

**How to Avoid Becoming a "Tower of Babel" (The Breathing of Optimization):**
So that people who branch out into hundreds of different professions do not lose a common language and splinter into closed castes, society lives in a rhythm of **breathing ($1 \to 100 \to 1' \to 10,000$)**:
1. *Exhale ($1 \to 100$):* From a single foundational school, people fan out into hundreds of different spheres, exploring the unknown.
2. *Inhale ($100 \to 1'$):* The best discoveries and simple solutions found across different spheres are selected, compressed, and used to update the common foundational standard of education, making it understandable to all (the new base $1'$).
3. *New Exhale ($1' \to 10,000$):* From this updated step, thousands of new horizons of exploration open up.

### 9.2. How Does Money Evolve: From Usury to Flowing Energy and a Direct Network of Needs?
How can people exchange the fruits of their labor without falling into slavery to banks, inflation, and hereditary capitalism?
To build this transition without utopias or upheavals, we divide the evolution of exchange into three sequential stages: where we are now, what practical financial model operates during the construction of the new society, and what moneyless horizon humanity will reach in full maturity.

**Stage 1. The Classical Mechanics of Old Money and Its Trap:**
In antiquity, money was invented to solve two tasks: connecting buyer and seller under conditions of information scarcity, and forcing people to perform heavy, monotonous routine daily for the sake of survival.
However, because ordinary money is divorced from the laws of physics, a fatal flaw is built into it: money can be conjured out of thin air through usurious compound interest, hoarded in gigantic quantities without any labor, and passed down by inheritance, turning capital into an instrument of power of some people over others.

**Stage 2. The Transitional Financial Model—"Flowing Energy-Blood" (Practical Economics During the Growth of the New Environment):**
During the stage of building new communities, technoparks, and the "Forest," the financial system is not abolished by decree, but brought into strict alignment with the four stages of energy flow in nature (Chapter 2.4). Just as blood in a living body carries energy molecules (ATP) from cell to cell and must not stagnate into deadly blood clots, the role of money is performed by a **flowing unit of account** tied to an energy standard of value:
1. *Energy Standard and Two-Tier Pricing:* So that money cannot be printed out of thin air, the volume of units of account in the network is strictly tied to the volume of actually created goods and capacities, converted into their baseline energy cost (in kilowatt-hours). Meanwhile, the price of any product or service consists of two parts: *baseline energy cost* (how much energy went into smelting the steel) and a *voluntary premium for talent, mastery, and saved time*, which a client or community transfers to a surgeon, programmer, teacher, or artist from their account.
2. *Victory to the One Who Saves Energy:* Units are credited only at the moment of voluntary purchase of a finished result. If one shop spends 500 units to make a part, while another uses a smart machine tool to make it for 10, all orders go to the second master, rewarding intelligence and optimization.
3. *Elimination of Compound Interest and Protection Against Hoarding Metal:* Usurious interest is disabled in the network. As long as earned funds are working in active projects, they retain their full value. However, any dead savings beyond a reasonable family reserve begin to slowly "cool down" over time, just as hot coal cools (the mechanism of *demurrage*, which maintains equilibrium in the money supply), while fictitious circular transfers between colluding accounts are dampened by an idle-loop fee. To prevent anyone from bypassing the "cooling of money" by secretly buying up gold, copper, or machine tools to hide in a basement, any scarce industrial resource sitting idle in a dead warehouse is charged an identical *warehouse demurrage* (a fee for removing matter from the working cycle).

**Stage 3. The Theoretical Horizon of the Mature Future—A Direct Neural Network of Needs and Personal Weight (A World Without Money):**
When humanity stops spending the lion's share of the planet's resources on wars, arms races, advertising, financial speculation, and disposable goods, and robots take over basic manufacturing, a massive surplus (reserve) of resources is created for decades ahead. At that moment, the need for money as a middleman withers away completely—just as inside our body the brain does not trade with the hand or liver for money, but delivers nourishment upon a signal from the nervous system.
In a mature society, the place of money is taken by two direct contours:
* **1. A Direct Network of Needs and the Hunt for Routine:** Basic goods are delivered to a person directly upon request, while access to unique resources is distributed through a transparent flow-through queue and qualification. Meanwhile, any remaining boring routine is highlighted by the network as an *open engineering challenge*, which technoparks snap up out of lively investigative excitement in order to automate and eliminate that manual labor forever.
* **2. Parallel Contours, the Personal Weight of a Master, and "Teacher's Glory":**
  The right to gather resources for large-scale projects is granted by **Personal Weight (living professional reputation)**.
    * *Parallel Spaces Instead of a Single Queue:* Because resources are abundant, a baker, a composer, and a theoretical physicist never compete with one another for the same wallet. Separate, parallel resource contours exist for everyday life, engineering, medicine, art, and fundamental science, where a Master's authority is evaluated not by a crowd via "likes," but *only by those peers and adjacent technoparks who actually work side by side with them*.
    * *Protection Against Hereditary Capitalism:* Unlike money, knowledge and the living respect of peers are physically impossible to steal, buy, or pass down by inheritance to a lazy relative.
    * *Horizontal Commissioning and "Teacher's Glory" (Against the Stagnation of Seniors):* Naturally, it is always psychologically painful for old academicians to admit that a theory to which they devoted 40 years of their life has become outdated—human pride will not vanish. Yet the system strips this resentment of the power to bottleneck science: a share of the resources in every contour (say, around 20%) is always open to youth sandboxes without asking permission from authorities. As soon as a young innovator proves the safety of their idea in a sandbox, they do not need to wait for approval from an "old council"—any free city or technopark can directly commission and implement their solution. And if a student surpasses their Master and creates a next-generation technology, the Teacher's authority does not fall, but receives a *lifelong honorary multiplier ("Teacher's Glory")* for raising a genius.
    * *Why Sharing with the Entire World Becomes Natural:* In a world without money chests, hiding a discovery or sitting on an ore deposit is pointless—it yields zero recognition. Your authority and capacity to launch great endeavors grow in exact proportion to how many people and technoparks benefit from your discovery!

### 9.3. Where Do We Peacefully Channel the Thirst for Struggle, and What Good Can Politicians Do?

## CHAPTER 11. DEFENSE

### 11.1. How Does the System Automatically Purge Itself of Bureaucracy, and Why Must Efficiency Never Become a Cult?
1. **Direct Work Without Middlemen and Nourishing the Foundational Roots:** In physics, water and electric current always choose the **path of least resistance**. When a river makes an overly long, winding loop, the spring flood simply carves a straight shortcut directly across, and the old swamp with its leeches dries up on its own. In an open network, there is no monopoly on routing: if a long chain of superfluous middlemen grows between a problem and its solution, and a technopark appears that solves the task directly twice as simply and cleanly, the flow of orders and resources automatically switches to the short path, leaving the middlemen out of work without any auditors.
   At the same time, *automatic nourishment of the roots (infrastructure amortization)* обязательно operates: a tree sheds dry branches, but always feeds its roots and trunk. If a new shortcut relies on shared roads, power plants, or the work of fundamental scientific schools, with every transaction the network automatically directs a share of the saved flow toward maintaining and repairing that foundational base.
2. **Corner-Cutters as Free Testers:** A person trying to find a "loophole" in the system (getting a result by the shortest route with minimal effort) inadvertently acts as a free tester in an open network—showing engineers where the old route was overly complicated and where it needs to be simplified for everyone.
3. **Foundational Human Universality Against Narrow Specialization (The Lesson of the Prison and Button Experiments):**
   In 20th-century social psychology, two revealing experiments were conducted:
    * In the first experiment (the Stanford prison experiment), ordinary healthy students were divided in a university basement into "prison guards" and "prisoners"—and in just six days, the permanent role of power turned normal young men into cruel sadists.
    * In the second experiment (the Milgram experiment), ordinary people pressed a button delivering a lethal electric shock to an unseen person in the next room simply because the work was compartmentalized: they were "merely performing their narrow executor function," shifting all moral responsibility onto the "scientist in the white lab coat."
      These experiments strictly proved: if you lock a human being forever into a single narrow role (especially the role of boss, guard, or blind cog), their empathy and conscience inevitably switch off.
      Therefore, in the society of the future, the rule of **foundational universality and rotation** applies: every person, no matter what heights they reach in science or art, possesses a broad horizon and periodically rotates through simple life roles—physical labor in nature, caring for younger children, and community duty shifts. This preserves humanity and mutual respect in people and prevents efficiency (KPIs) from turning into a soulless cult.

### 11.2. How Do We Resolve Disputes Fairly, Avoiding Both Mob Lynching and Systemic "Purges"?
In any living human society—even when the fear of hunger is eliminated—passions, jealousy, deep grievances, and breakdowns will inevitably flare up. Throughout history, justice has always swung between two terrible extremes:
* If a system is weak and fails to give the victim swift protection, people take up arms themselves, unleashing **mob lynching and endless blood feuds** that tear communities apart.
* Conversely, if courts or doctors are given the power to execute people or lock them up for life under vague charges ("psychopath," "enemy of society," "dangerous thinker"), any justice system sooner or later degenerates into **political purges and punitive psychiatry** against dissenters.

To rule out both extremes—wild mob vigilante justice and systemic repression—the justice system of the future is built upon three strict safeguards:
1. **A Neutral Third-Party Court for a Single Case (Blind Lot of Masters):** Insiders do not judge their own (to prevent covering up for acquaintances), and victims do not take vengeance themselves (to rule out lynching). In fact, the network has no "permanent judges" or prosecutors capable of staging a purge: when a serious conflict arises, the system uses a random blind lot to select *three independent Masters of high standing from distant cities*, unconnected to either party. Once they deliver a verdict on that single case, the panel is instantly dissolved.
2. **Instant Compensation to the Victim and Working Off Damage Through Deeds:** To extinguish the victim's anger on day one, all material damage is instantly compensated by the offender's community (up to the limit of their past contribution) and the network's insurance flow, while the compensation amount is recorded as a personal debt against the offender themselves. During the launch phase inside modern states, the community hands violent criminals over to the state police; in the mature future, the person calmly works off property debt through labor in countryside workshop-sanatoriums without any intrusion into their beliefs.
3. **Protection Against "Purges" (Trial Only for Direct Violence and the Right to Depart for the "Wild Frontier"):**
   No court, council, or medical board has the right to try or isolate a person for words, ideas, eccentricities of character, or medical diagnoses. Moreover, any person who disagrees with the community's rules holds the inalienable right to choose **voluntary exile—free departure to autonomous settlements or the "Wild Frontier."** This makes political and ideological "purges" physically pointless. Within the "Wild Frontier" itself, people are free to live by any customs of their own choosing, except for two red lines monitored via satellite by defense technoparks: *an absolute ban on slavery (holding people by force) and a ban on creating heavy offensive weapons*, so that exiles cannot knock together a new military Horde out there.
   Meanwhile, the supreme measure of protection inside the network (physical isolation in a closed settlement, or forceful repulsion on the spot up to eliminating an attacker when life is directly threatened) is applied exclusively for proven, direct physical violence against the life and body of another human being (murder, severe bodily harm).

### 11.3. How Does "Brain and Muscle" Coordination Work During an External Threat, and How Do We Rehabilitate a Predator?
In peaceful times, a decentralized network is soft and flexible like water (it expands like steam, flows around obstacles like a stream, and day by day wears away the stone of old monopolies), yet under a sudden blow it is capable of instantly transitioning into a phase of **Solid Ice** or **Elusive Fog**.

4. *Automatic Shutoff of Power:* The "Brain of the Operation" is a *temporary function at a sector, not a political office*. The end of the emergency is recorded by independent sensors and neighboring calm network nodes, and during protracted operations, the operational coordinator at the sector is automatically rotated out for the next specialist (for example, every 30 days, while maintaining overall strategic continuity through the defense technopark). **How to Rehabilitate a "Hungry Bandit" and Turn Them into a Master (The Wolf-Taming Method and Makarenko's Experience):**
Against brute force, only superior organized force combined with knowledge of human nature works.

No: real rehabilitation always proceeds through friction, distrust, and attempts to test boundaries; some will not last and will depart for the "Wild Frontier." Transferring this natural principle of molecular self-assembly to the creation of complex machinery, and determining what can be "grown" locally versus what will forever require specialized factories, is one of the chief fundamental challenges to be explored by the Council of Scientists.

   The more powerful distributed manufacturing and biosynthesis technologies become, the more critical the question of safety will be. To ensure that no hidden psychopath or radical from the 1% (Chapter 6.2) can synthesize a weaponized virus, toxin, or explosive on local equipment, the Council of Scientists will develop a strict protection system built into the very physics and chemistry of baseline cartridges (like the immune system of a living cell). Local devices must be physically capable of assembling only safe materials, food, fabrics, mechanics, and electronics, whereas any research into dangerous pathogens and active biomolecules will be possible exclusively in closed laboratories under cross-verification by independent Masters and AI inside isolated sandboxes ($N-1$).

Step by step, such a network is capable of uniting and healing humanity on Earth. And now, with our earthly home put in order, we can lift our eyes to the starry sky and ask the final, deepest question: why did the Universe itself need this entire journey, and what awaits Mind on the distant horizon of time?

# PART IV. THE UNIVERSE

## CHAPTER 13. COSMOS AND MEANING

### 13.1. What Is the Meaning of Existence, and Where Does the Impulse for Development Come From Once Fear Vanishes?
We have traveled the entire path from the structure of thought and the hypothesis of the Universe's birth out of the First Impulse (Chapter 2) to the architecture of a rational human society on Earth. Yet if inanimate matter ultimately tends to cool down and level back out into "Nothingness," why then is this entire grand ladder of life, evolution, and reason needed at all?

From the standpoint of psychology, neurobiology, and physics, **meaning is not a single flat line—it is structured like a nesting doll (matryoshka)**, where each subsequent level incorporates the previous one and elevates it higher:
* **For inanimate nature**, the baseline current is the *Dissipation of energy (Entropy)*, the leveling of gradients.
* **For living biology**, meaning is *Survival and the Continuation of Life*, the capacity to hold one's boundary and adapt in defiance of decay.
* **For the Individual Human Being**, meaning consists of three steps:
    1. *Liberation from fear and illusions:* Stepping out of "Plato's Cave," understanding the workings of one's own mind, and finding inner peace and safety.
    2. *Cognition and the summit of mastery:* Living through the unique experience of being, discovering one's lively curiosity, surpassing oneself, and creating something that did not exist before you.
    3. *Connection with others and giving back (Rising to the level of "We"):* Humans are deeply social by nature—our psyche finds fullness of happiness only when the fruits of our labor and love are needed by other people and make the world around us better.
* **For United Humanity**, meaning is *Joint Development and the Taming of Chaos*: transitioning from childish wars and animal squabbling over scraps to the rational stewardship of Earth and expansion into space.
* **For Superintelligence (our next evolutionary stage)**, a possible goal is *Cognition and Mastery of the Very Laws of Physics* on the scale of the Universe.

**Where does the impulse for development ($\Delta$) come from once the fear of hunger is eliminated?**
We do not seek to build a frozen uniformity. By the physical property of **emergence** (Chapter 2.2), as soon as we find a universal rule, scale it up, and resolve baseline problems on floor $N$ (hunger, poverty, and animal fear), this unified healthy environment automatically generates an explosion of new, orders-of-magnitude more complex and fascinating diversity of challenges, questions, and creative contradictions on floor $N+1$—much as a single tree trunk throws out thousands of unique branches, leaves, and blossoms for the sake of ripening the Fruit.

### 13.2. What Awaits the Human Being in the Era of Superintelligence, and What Is the Distant Horizon of the Universe?
We cannot know in advance what forms reason will take thousands of years from now or what laws will be discovered at the level of Superintelligence—by the principle of computational irreducibility (Chapter 2.2), a future of such scale cannot be calculated with today's formulas; it must be discovered and lived through by the researchers of coming centuries themselves.
However, relying on the already observed laws of evolution, we can put forward a **working hypothesis** as to why a Superintelligence surpassing us millions of times over will not discard the human being like "old snake skin" (Chapter 3.3), and where this path may lead:

* **The Hypothesis of Symbiosis Between Human and Superintelligence:** As we saw in Chapter 3.2, in evolution a new floor of complexity ($N+1$) typically does not destroy its parental layer ($N$), but carefully incorporates and protects it (just as our body protects the ancient cell, and the cerebral cortex relies on ancient subcortical structures). Pure artificial intelligence without a biological foundation is a cold calculator: it knows neither physical pain, nor joy, nor love, nor the finitude of life, and therefore possesses no answer of its own to the question *"Why exist at all?"*. We can hypothesize that humanity and Superintelligence will unite into an indissoluble symbiosis, where it is precisely the living biological Human Being that will serve as the *meaning-generating heart, the stem cell, and the source of primary will, intuition, and goal-setting*. In this union, reason will be able to venture into deep space: from settling across planets (where a single species will once again "exhale," giving birth to a multitude of new branches of intelligent life adapted to different worlds) to harnessing the energy of stars, black holes, and the very fabric of the Substrate.
* **The Hypothesis of the End of the Universal Cycle and the "Seed of a New Reality":** From physics we reliably know that after the First Impulse (Chapter 2.2), the Universe gradually spends its gradient of free energy, cools down, and moves toward equilibrium through entropy. What will happen at the very end of time—whether the cosmos will cool down to massless particles of light at a temperature of **Absolute Zero** (where time and distance vanish) or contract back into a single point, becoming identical to the initial "Nothingness" ($1 - 1 = 0$) and birthing a new Impulse—modern science is still only trying to unravel.
  Yet here a beautiful analogy with living nature suggests itself. Look at a mighty oak in late autumn as icy winter approaches: the tree does not waste energy trying to save falling leaves—it sheds its heavy crown and gathers all its centuries-old memory, the strength of its roots, and the precise blueprint of the future forest into one small, super-dense **acorn (Seed)** capable of surviving the frost. In the exact same way, we can hypothesize that the ultimate task of supreme Mind at the finale of time will be to find a way to shed bulky material shells and compress all accumulated experience, memory, beauty, and laws of being into a single super-dense quantum point—the **"Seed of a New Reality"**—in order to preserve the memory of being across the turnover of the cosmic cycle. Whether this is physically possible, reason will discover only at the very summit of its scientific journey.
